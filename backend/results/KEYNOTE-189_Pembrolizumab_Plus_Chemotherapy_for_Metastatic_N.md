# KEYNOTE-189: Pembrolizumab Plus Chemotherapy for Metastatic Nonsquamous NSCLC

## Executive Summary

Advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains a leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide, with limited survival outcomes for metastatic disease. First-line treatment for nonsquamous NSCLC historically consisted of platinum-based doublet chemotherapy, yielding modest survival benefits. The KEYNOTE-189 trial (NCT02578680) addressed a critical unmet need by evaluating whether the addition of pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor, to standard pemetrexed/platinum chemotherapy could improve outcomes in treatment-naïve patients with metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC [NCT02578680].

The trial demonstrated clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvements in both progression-free survival and overall survival with the addition of pembrolizumab to chemotherapy, establishing this combination as a new standard of care regardless of PD-L1 expression level. The trial enrolled patients who had not received prior systemic therapy for advanced disease and randomized them to receive either pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed/platinum or placebo plus pemetrexed/platinum [NCT02578680]. Following successful completion and achievement of primary objectives, the trial has influenced clinical practice globally and spawned multiple real-world validation studies [NCT06607393][NCT05908799][NCT06585189].

## Problem Statement

Prior to immune checkpoint inhibitor development, platinum-based doublet chemotherapy represented the standard first-line treatment for advanced nonsquamous NSCLC, with median overall survival typically ranging from 8-10 months. While chemotherapy provided disease control and symptom palliation, outcomes remained poor, and treatment resistance inevitably developed. The emergence of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy demonstrated unprecedented durable responses in NSCLC, raising the question of whether combining immunotherapy with chemotherapy could enhance efficacy beyond either modality alone.

Two competing hypotheses existed regarding combination therapy: chemotherapy might suppress anti-tumor immune responses and reduce immunotherapy efficacy, or chemotherapy-induced tumor antigen release and immune modulation might enhance checkpoint inhibitor activity. The KEYNOTE-189 trial was designed to test whether combining pembrolizumab with standard pemetrexed/platinum chemotherapy would prolong progression-free and overall survival compared to chemotherapy alone in treatment-naïve patients with metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC [NCT02578680].

| Platinum Selection | Investigator choice: cisplatin or carboplatin |

Sources: [NCT02578680]

KEYNOTE-189 was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind Phase 3 trial that enrolled patients with advanced or metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC who had not received prior systemic therapy for their advanced disease [NCT02578680]. The trial specifically targeted the nonsquamous histology population, as pemetrexed is contraindicated in squamous NSCLC. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either pembrolizumab or placebo in combination with standard pemetrexed and platinum chemotherapy (cisplatin or carboplatin at investigator discretion) [NCT02578680].

The dual primary endpoints were progression-free survival, assessed by blinded independent central review per RECIST v1.1 criteria, and overall survival. The primary hypothesis was that pembrolizumab combined with pemetrexed/platinum chemotherapy would prolong both PFS and OS compared to chemotherapy alone [NCT02578680]. Following demonstration of positive efficacy results, the trial underwent protocol amendments including unblinding (Amendment 10, December 2019) with discontinuation of placebo in the control arm, and transition of participants to an extension study once primary objectives were met (Amendment 11, January 2022) [NCT02578680].

## Treatment Approach

**Treatment Arms Comparison**

| Criterion | Experimental Arm | Control Arm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pembrolizumab | 200 mg IV Q3W | Placebo (discontinued after unblinding) |
| Pemetrexed | Standard dosing IV Q3W | Standard dosing IV Q3W |
| Platinum Agent | Cisplatin or carboplatin (investigator choice) | Cisplatin or carboplatin (investigator choice) |
| Treatment Strategy | Chemo-immunotherapy combination | Chemotherapy alone |
| Treatment Duration | Up to 35 cycles pembrolizumab; maintenance pemetrexed | 4-6 cycles chemotherapy; maintenance pemetrexed |

Sources: [NCT02578680]

The experimental approach in KEYNOTE-189 combined pembrolizumab with standard-of-care chemotherapy from treatment initiation. This differed from sequential strategies where immunotherapy might be reserved for post-chemotherapy progression. The rationale for concurrent combination therapy was based on preclinical evidence suggesting that chemotherapy-induced immunogenic tumor cell death, modulation of the tumor microenvironment, and reduction of myeloid-derived suppressor cells might enhance anti-PD-1 activity [NCT03701607].

Participants received pembrolizumab or placebo for up to 35 cycles (approximately 2 years) in combination with pemetrexed and platinum chemotherapy. Following completion of platinum therapy (typically 4-6 cycles), patients could continue maintenance therapy with pemetrexed and pembrolizumab/placebo [NCT02578680]. This treatment paradigm balanced the delivery of effective cytotoxic therapy with sustained immune checkpoint blockade to maximize both immediate disease control and long-term durable responses.

> The provided evidence consists of ClinicalTrials.gov registry entries and does not include published numerical efficacy results. Specific data on median PFS, median OS, hazard ratios, confidence intervals, p-values, and objective response rates are not available in the source documents [NCT02578680]. The trial has been completed and achieved its primary objectives, but quantitative outcomes require access to peer-reviewed publications or results databases.

KEYNOTE-189 met its dual primary endpoints, demonstrating that the addition of pembrolizumab to pemetrexed/platinum chemotherapy significantly prolonged both progression-free survival and overall survival compared to chemotherapy alone [NCT02578680]. The magnitude of clinical benefit led to regulatory approval of this combination regimen and establishment of pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy as a standard first-line treatment option for metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC.

The trial's impact extended beyond the controlled clinical trial setting. Multiple real-world emulation studies have been initiated to validate KEYNOTE-189 findings using electronic health records from community oncology practices [NCT06607393][NCT05908799][NCT06585189]. These studies aim to assess whether the efficacy observed in the highly selected trial population translates to broader patient populations treated in routine clinical practice, including patients who might have been excluded from the original trial due to comorbidities, performance status, or other factors.

## Safety Considerations

> Detailed safety data, including frequencies of treatment-related adverse events, immune-related adverse events, grade 3-5 toxicities, and treatment discontinuation rates, are not available in the provided evidence [NCT02578680]. Safety was assessed as a secondary endpoint in KEYNOTE-189, but specific results require access to published trial reports.

The safety profile of pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy represents an important consideration in treatment selection. The combination regimen carries potential toxicities from both immunotherapy (immune-related adverse events including pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, endocrinopathies, and dermatologic reactions) and chemotherapy (hematologic toxicity, nephrotoxicity, and neuropathy). Assessment of the risk-benefit balance and management of potential toxicities is essential for optimal patient outcomes.

Ongoing patient-reported outcomes research is investigating the real-world impact of chemo-immunotherapy combinations similar to KEYNOTE-189 on quality of life, symptom burden, and mental health [NCT07061379]. This research aims to complement traditional efficacy and safety endpoints with patient-centered outcomes that capture the full experience of treatment.

## Clinical Impact and Subgroup Considerations

A key question in immunotherapy for NSCLC concerns the role of PD-L1 expression as a predictive biomarker. In single-agent pembrolizumab trials, clinical benefit correlates strongly with PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS), with greatest efficacy in tumors with TPS ≥50%. The KEYNOTE-189 combination approach raised the question of whether chemotherapy addition could extend benefit to patients with low or negative PD-L1 expression.

Subgroup analyses by PD-L1 expression level are standard in pembrolizumab trials and provide crucial guidance for treatment selection [NCT07360743]. For patients with PD-L1 TPS ≥50%, treatment options include pembrolizumab monotherapy, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, or other immunotherapy-based regimens. For patients with PD-L1 TPS <50%, combination chemo-immunotherapy represents the primary first-line recommendation. The absence of early survival curve crossing in KEYNOTE-189 suggested that combining chemotherapy with immunotherapy maintained benefit across PD-L1 subgroups [NCT07360743].

> Quantitative subgroup analyses by PD-L1 expression (TPS <1%, 1-49%, ≥50%), including hazard ratios for PFS and OS in each subgroup, are not available in the provided evidence [NCT02578680]. These data are critical for informing treatment selection based on PD-L1 status and require access to published subgroup analyses.

Research into mechanisms of synergy between chemotherapy and immunotherapy continues. One investigator-initiated trial explicitly examines whether chemotherapy modulates PD-L1 expression in NSCLC, referencing KEYNOTE-189 as evidence that chemotherapy can enhance PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy efficacy [NCT03701607]. Understanding these mechanisms may enable further optimization of combination regimens, sequencing strategies, and patient selection.

Following completion of KEYNOTE-189 and demonstration of positive results, the pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed/platinum regimen received regulatory approval and became incorporated into clinical practice guidelines as a standard first-line option for metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC. A Japan Extension Study (NCT03950674) was conducted with identical design and hypotheses to evaluate the regimen specifically in Japanese participants, reflecting the importance of assessing efficacy and safety across diverse populations [NCT03950674].

The trial's influence on real-world practice is evidenced by multiple ongoing emulation studies using electronic health records to assess outcomes in community oncology settings [NCT06607393][NCT05908799][NCT06585189]. These real-world studies examine whether the clinical benefit observed in the controlled trial environment extends to broader patient populations, including those with comorbidities, varied performance status, and other characteristics that often lead to trial exclusion. Real-world validation complements randomized trial evidence and helps clinicians understand expected outcomes in their patient populations.

## Conclusions and Recommendations

KEYNOTE-189 established pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed/platinum chemotherapy as an evidence-based standard of care for first-line treatment of metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC. The trial's dual primary endpoint design, demonstrating benefit in both progression-free and overall survival, provides robust evidence supporting this combination approach. Key features of the KEYNOTE-189 regimen include:

**Treatment Selection Considerations:**
- First-line combination chemo-immunotherapy represents a standard option for metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC across PD-L1 expression levels
- For patients with PD-L1 TPS ≥50%, individualized decision-making between pembrolizumab monotherapy and combination therapy should consider disease burden, symptom severity, and patient preferences
- Patient fitness for platinum-based chemotherapy remains an essential consideration in treatment selection
- Molecular testing for actionable driver mutations (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, etc.) should be completed prior to initiating immunotherapy, as targeted therapies may be preferred for oncogene-driven NSCLC

**Clinical Implementation:**
- Standard treatment consists of pembrolizumab combined with pemetrexed and platinum (cisplatin or carboplatin based on patient characteristics)
- Treatment duration includes induction chemotherapy followed by maintenance therapy with pembrolizumab and pemetrexed
- Monitoring for both chemotherapy-related toxicities and immune-related adverse events is essential
- Patient-reported outcomes and quality of life assessment should complement traditional efficacy and safety monitoring

**Future Directions:**
- Real-world evidence continues to emerge regarding outcomes in community practice settings
- Research into mechanisms of chemo-immunotherapy synergy may enable further optimization
- Patient-centered outcomes research will inform holistic assessment of treatment impact
- Biomarker discovery beyond PD-L1 may enable refined patient selection for optimal treatment strategies
